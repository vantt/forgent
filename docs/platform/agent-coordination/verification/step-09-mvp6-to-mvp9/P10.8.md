### Gap 1 — no step kind reaches `linkSessionContribution` at all

`run.mjs`'s public request vocabulary (the only thing
`runGroupThinkingRequest` ever forwards into) is closed over exactly four
step kinds: `operation`, `authorize`, `disposition`, `fan-out`
(`schema.mjs`'s own `validateSteps`). **There is no step kind that reaches
`linkSessionContribution`** — the one door that (a) records a durable,
evidence-preserving contribution in the Deliberation Contribution Ledger
(P08.1-P08.3), and (b) is the ONLY place Delphi-Feedback-Lite's own round
**ORDER** property is actually enforced (P10.4.md §2: the `round2-open`
visibility-window check lives inside `linkSessionContribution`, never inside
`dispatchDeclaredOperation` for a `required`-activation binding, which is
what every `propose-round1`/`propose-round2` binding in this fixture is).

Confirmed live, not just read from source: this file's own third test sends
a `{type: 'contribution', ...}` step through `runGroupThinkingRequest` and
gets back `StoreError('validation', 'steps[0].type must be "operation",
"fan-out", "authorize", or "disposition"')` — the schema itself refuses
any step kind outside those four, unconditionally.

**This is a shared gap across all three group-thinking-lite protocols**
(RFC-Review-Lite, Nominal-Group-Lite, Delphi-Feedback-Lite all declare
`contributions.allowedTypes[]` on their operations and rely on
`linkSessionContribution` for lineage), not a Delphi-specific defect — named
here because THIS cell's own Acceptance criteria depend on it most directly
(round order, and the "private input -> mediated aggregate" evidence-
preserving lineage claim). **Cross-confirmed independently**: P10.6's own
Doer/Reviewer/Red-Team found and documented the identical gap for
RFC-Review-Lite (`P10.6.md` §1, in the sibling worktree
`step-09-mvp6-to-mvp9-p10-6`) at essentially the same time, from a
completely separate investigation, and the Coordinator has already flagged
it pack-wide in that track's `current-cell.md` ("a real, PACK-WIDE finding —
contribution-typed lineage is unreachable through the pack gate for all
three registered protocols... flagged as a candidate 'shared missing
primitive' for P10.10 to formally judge"). This is not this cell's own
isolated read of the code — it is independently reproduced by a second,
unrelated cell against a different protocol.

**Consequence for this cell's own scope, stated plainly:**
- The round **CAP** (`maxRounds: 2` on the shared topology edge) is fully
  re-provable through the pack gate — it is enforced inside
  `dispatchDeclaredOperation` itself, reached by an ordinary `operation`
  step. Proved (§3, test 2).
- The round **ORDER** property is **not reachable through the pack gate at
  all** — not because the pack weakens the underlying guarantee (nothing
  about `group-thinking-pack.mjs` removes or bypasses
  `linkSessionContribution`'s own refusal, which P10.4 already proved
  directly and unconditionally, and remains true regardless of this cell's
  own findings), but because the public surface this cell was asked to
  drive the chain through has no door to the mechanism where that refusal
  lives. Proved as a genuine dispatch-level PARITY result instead (§3, test
  3): dispatching `propose-round2` through the pack before `aggregate` has
  settled succeeds identically to P10.4's own direct-call finding — the pack
  introduces no NEW bypass — and the missing-step-kind gap is demonstrated
  concretely in the same test.
- The full chain's CONTRIBUTION lineage (anchors, roundKey — what P10.4's
  own end-to-end test reconstructs from `replayed.contributions`) is
  genuinely unreachable through the pack. What IS provable and proved (§3,
  test 1) is the DISPATCH lineage (assignments, actor-scope tier
  provenance) — and the explicit, asserted fact that dispatch-only through
  the pack links zero contributions (`replayed.contributions` is `[]`).

### Gap 2 — the pack's own unconditional close-on-quorum auto-closes a multi-round session EARLIER than "after aggregate," blocking even `aggregate` itself across a call boundary

`run.mjs` always attempts `closeSessionByQuorum` at the end of every single
request (P10.1.md's own bypass #5 reasoning: this is deliberate,
security-relevant behavior, not a defect). `classifySessionQuorum` closes a
session to `'completed'` the moment **every** declared actor
(`facilitator-actor`, `panelist-a`, `panelist-b`) has at least ONE settled,
non-failed Assignment — it has no notion of "aggregate and round 2 are still
owed" for a multi-round protocol.

**Sharpened after a cross-cell prompt, and empirically re-verified — this is
more severe than first documented.** P10.7's own Doer found the identical
root mechanism for Nominal-Group-Lite and asked every sibling cell to check
its own protocol directly rather than trust the first write-up. Re-checked
here with a live probe and a new committed test (§3 of the Proof Matrix,
"the auto-close boundary is EARLIER than 'after aggregate'"): the session
closes to `'completed'` the moment **convene + BOTH round-1 proposals**
settle — **before `aggregate` is ever dispatched**, not merely before
round-2 as this report first said. A second `runGroupThinkingRequest` call
attempting only `aggregate` after that point is refused outright:
`session "..." is not active (status: "completed")`. This means a real
driver that pauses between round-1 and aggregate for ANY reason (e.g. a
human reviewing both private proposals before the facilitator synthesizes
them) can never resume through the pack at all — not "resume is harder than
expected," but "the back half of the protocol becomes structurally
unreachable across that specific call boundary."

**This does NOT undermine this cell's own single-call full-chain proof
(§2/§3, first test) — verified directly, not assumed.** A second new test
(`"...does NOT undermine this file's own single-call full-chain proof..."`)
drives the identical six-step chain in ONE call and confirms `result.closed
=== true` only at the very end, with every step (including `aggregate` and
both round-2 proposals) genuinely dispatched first. The reason, read
directly from `run.mjs`'s own source: `closeSessionByQuorum` is called
exactly once, strictly OUTSIDE and AFTER the `for (const step of
request.steps)` loop — never per-step, never mid-loop. So within one call,
every declared step dispatches before quorum is evaluated even once for
that call; the early-close risk is a cross-CALL boundary problem only, never
a within-call one. This is the direct, verified answer to the question
"does your own full-chain proof only work because you happened to drive it
in one single call": yes, and the source-level reason that shape is
genuinely safe (not merely lucky) is now stated and tested explicitly.

This is a second, separate, real finding from Gap 1: an implicit,
pack-inherited close-eagerness that narrows WHERE a multi-round protocol can
actually be interrupted and resumed through this surface — and for Delphi
specifically, the earliest safe interruption+resume point is BEFORE every
actor has dispatched even once (used by this file's own resume test, with
the reasoning recorded in that test's own comment). Worth the Coordinator's
full attention for P10.10, cross-referenced against P10.7's identical
finding for Nominal-Group-Lite: any future multi-round protocol driven
through this pack across more than one call needs its own resume points
chosen with this quorum behavior in mind, never assumed safe from the
protocol's own round/phase structure alone.

## 6. Gaps

- **Gap 1 (§1): the group-thinking pack's public request vocabulary has no
  step kind reaching `linkSessionContribution`.** This is the cell's
  central, honestly-named finding — a genuine kernel/pack-surface gap, not
  an implementation bug this cell could fix (every file it would require
  touching — `run.mjs`, `schema.mjs`, `group-thinking-pack.mjs` — is
  Do-Not-Touch for this cell). Consequence: the Deliberation Contribution
  Ledger (P08.1-P08.3) — and therefore Delphi-Feedback-Lite's own round-
  ORDER enforcement and its full lineage-with-anchors claim — is reachable
  through DIRECT engine calls only, never through the public pack/skill
  surface Phase 10 built. Affects RFC-Review-Lite and Nominal-Group-Lite
  identically (both declare `contributions.allowedTypes[]` on their own
  operations); the Coordinator should treat this as a shared finding across
  P10.6/P10.7/P10.8, not resolve it three times independently. Per
  current-cell.md's own instruction and this repo's Do-Not-Touch discipline,
  this is reported as a blocking finding for P10.10 to classify (most likely
  "shared missing primitive," per Phase 10's own Cells text: "If a shared
  missing primitive is proven, do not hide it in the Protocol Pack or skill.
  Leave Step 09 open with a named proposal and evidence"), never patched
  silently by this cell.
- **Gap 2 (§1): unconditional close-on-quorum auto-closes a multi-round
  session as soon as every actor has ONE settled Assignment**, regardless of
  how many rounds/phases a protocol declares. Sharpened after a cross-cell
  prompt from P10.7 (which found the same mechanism for Nominal-Group-Lite)
  and re-verified live for Delphi-Feedback-Lite specifically: the real
  boundary is right after round-1 completes for BOTH panelists — BEFORE
  `aggregate` ever dispatches, not merely before round-2 as this report
  first said. A driver that pauses between round-1 and aggregate for any
  reason can never resume through the pack; `aggregate` itself, not just
  round-2, becomes unreachable across that call boundary. Verified NOT to
  undermine this cell's own single-call full-chain proof (a dedicated new
  test proves why: `closeSessionByQuorum` fires exactly once, strictly after
  `run.mjs`'s entire per-call steps loop, never mid-loop, so every step in
  ONE call dispatches before quorum is evaluated even once for that call).
  Narrows where a multi-round protocol can be interrupted/resumed through
  the pack across MULTIPLE calls; did not block this cell's own resume proof
  (a working split point exists — before every actor has dispatched even
  once — and is used), but is worth P10.10's full attention, read alongside
  P10.7's identical finding for Nominal-Group-Lite, for any multi-call
  driving pattern against this surface.
