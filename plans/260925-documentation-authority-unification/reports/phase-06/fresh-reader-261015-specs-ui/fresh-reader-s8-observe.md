## Scenario A: add `friction reopen`

- Q1 What to read first: Start at the switchboard row for Observe to find the owner (`docs/specs/observe.md`, out of my scope). For orientation, read the candidate spec sections 1 (Owns) and 4 (Commands & Routing) and the friction lane. The friction lane is only a stub that points at phase F5 (cites: `docs/platform/documentation/switchboard.md#2-area-authority-routes`, `docs/platform/observe/spec.md#1-overview-boundaries`, `docs/platform/observe/spec.md#friction-làn-b`).
- Q2 What kind of work is this: A new observable command on an existing area. It extends the friction subcommand set, which currently lists `ping, record, resolve, list, show, rank` with no `reopen`. The record lifecycle (resolved to open again) is not described in the files in scope. The friction lane stub gives no state model, so I cannot verify whether reopen is allowed, or how resolve is stored (cites: `docs/platform/observe/spec.md#4-commands-routing`, `docs/platform/observe/spec.md#friction-làn-b`).
- Q3 What contract does it touch: The `fgos friction` native subcommand surface in Rust, the `observe.friction.v1` versioned contract (named in the contracts lane), the append-only writer shards under `.fgos/observe/friction/<writerId>.jsonl`, and the golden fixtures. The decision record says friction is append-only and that the Rust host is the only writer. A reopen would have to be a new appended event, not an edit. I cannot confirm from scope whether `observe.friction.v1` has a status/event shape that allows it (cites: `docs/platform/observe/spec.md#contract-quyết-định-làn-c-f8`, `docs/platform/observe/decisions/observe-component-architecture.md#d-adr0037-observe-component-architecture`).
- Q4 How much risk: Moderate. It is additive and stays inside Observe, with no Work coupling. The risks are a versioned contract change, a fixture regeneration, the `nativeOnly` routing, and writing only through the Rust host. Edits to `docs/specs/observe.md` are a frozen legacy root and need a reviewed ratchet exception (cites: `docs/platform/migration-authoring-rules.md#2-legacy-roots-ratchet-and-exceptions`, `docs/platform/observe/decisions/observe-component-architecture.md#d-adr0037-observe-component-architecture`).
- Q5 What proof means done: The spec names the verification surfaces: golden fixtures in `test/fixtures/observe/` generated by the Rust and Node CLIs via `scripts/regenerate-observe-fixtures.mjs`, the versioned contracts, and doctor checks. Tests for the Rust crate and the CLI envelope (`fgos.v1`) are implied. The candidate carries no certified test commands, so I cannot state an exact test command from scope (cites: `docs/platform/observe/spec.md#contract-quyết-định-làn-c-f8`, `docs/platform/observe/spec.md#3-storage-model`).
- Q6 What learning gets left behind: Update the single current owner once. That is `docs/specs/observe.md` (via a reviewed exception), and do not dual-author the same claim in the candidate spec. Record candidate impact in the migration ledger or phase notes. Decision history goes in the owner's decision history. The existing decision record is D-ADR0037 and is candidate-copy only (cites: `docs/platform/migration-authoring-rules.md#1-core-principles`, `docs/platform/documentation/switchboard.md#1-operating-invariants`).

## Scenario B: `fgos show <work-id>` displays related friction records

- Q1 What to read first: The decision record D-ADR0037, which concerns exactly this. Then the spec's "Must Not Own" section and the switchboard row for work-state, which routes `show` to `docs/specs/work-state.md`. That file is out of scope, and `docs/io-contract.md` owns the CLI I/O contract (out of scope too) (cites: `docs/platform/observe/decisions/observe-component-architecture.md#d-adr0037-observe-component-architecture`, `docs/platform/observe/spec.md#must-not-own`, `docs/platform/documentation/switchboard.md#2-area-authority-routes`).
- Q2 What kind of work is this: A direction reversal, not a simple feature. D-ADR0037 settled that Work stops reading friction, that the friction CLI does not join Work state, and that the `frictions` section was removed from `list`/`show` output. This request re-adds that coupling (cites: `docs/platform/observe/decisions/observe-component-architecture.md#d-adr0037-observe-component-architecture`).
- Q3 What contract does it touch: The `show` output envelope owned by work-state and the io-contract (owners not verifiable in scope). It also touches Observe's boundary rule that Observe must not make other components depend on it, since the composition root (`apps/fgos`) wires data sources. The decision says "Work ngừng đọc friction" (Work no longer reads friction). `work:<id>` is already a valid friction subject kind owned by work-state, so a subject-based lookup through `fgos friction list` may exist, but whether friction subjects can carry `work:<id>` is not stated in scope (cites: `docs/platform/observe/spec.md#1-overview-boundaries`, `docs/platform/observe/spec.md#2-shared-entities-subjects`).
- Q4 How much risk: High for design, moderate for code. It undoes an explicit settled decision and cross-couples two components. Per the review rules the owner or user must decide before reversal. The decision record is a candidate copy of legacy text, so the binding statement of D-ADR0037 sits in `docs/specs/observe.md`'s decision history, which I could not read. A cheaper alternative is to leave `show` unchanged and have users run `fgos friction list/show`. That is a trade-off to put to the owner, not something the files decide (cites: `docs/platform/observe/decisions/observe-component-architecture.md#d-adr0037-observe-component-architecture`, `docs/platform/governance.md#4-authority-matrix`).
- Q5 What proof means done: Not derivable from the files in scope. The authority matrix says a Decision establishes provenance and supersession and must not silently rewrite historical intent, so done needs a superseding decision plus changed tests on the `show` envelope. I cannot name the exact test suite (cites: `docs/platform/governance.md#4-authority-matrix`).
- Q6 What learning gets left behind: A new decision that supersedes the relevant D-ADR0037 bullets (Work stops reading friction, `frictions` dropped from `show`), recorded in the legacy owner spec once. Do not rewrite the old decision in place. The component-boundary check is also relevant, since the observe portal says to read `docs/platform/component-boundary.md` before changing runtime boundaries (the file is out of scope) (cites: `docs/platform/governance.md#4-authority-matrix`, `docs/platform/observe/README.md#added-in-candidate-verification-and-change-boundary`).

## Scenario C: stop tracking observe shard files in git and change the store lock expiry

- Q1 What to read first: The candidate spec sections 3 (Storage Model) and 1, then D-ADR0037, which states the tracked-shard decision. Then the switchboard owner row (`docs/specs/observe.md`) (cites: `docs/platform/observe/spec.md#3-storage-model`, `docs/platform/observe/decisions/observe-component-architecture.md#d-adr0037-observe-component-architecture`).
- Q2 What kind of work is this: Two storage-model changes, both reversing or altering settled decisions. First, "tracked shard per writer": the files are tracked in git, with migration by `(src, seq)` cursor. Second, the lock TTL of 30 seconds (with automatic reclaim when the holder is dead). Both appear in the spec and the decision (cites: `docs/platform/observe/spec.md#3-storage-model`, `docs/platform/observe/decisions/observe-component-architecture.md#d-adr0037-observe-component-architecture`).
- Q3 What contract does it touch: The `.fgos/observe/<store>/<writerId>.jsonl` shard layout and the `.fgos/observe/.lock` record shape and TTL. It also touches the doctor check `observe-dir-writable` and the golden fixtures. The tracked-shard choice is why per-writer files exist (merge-friendly under git). Untracking would remove the reason for that split, and I cannot verify from scope what else depends on tracked history. A separate repo rule I could not read may restrict commits under `.fgos/`, so I do not claim it (cites: `docs/platform/observe/spec.md#3-storage-model`, `docs/platform/observe/spec.md#contract-quyết-định-làn-c-f8`).
- Q4 How much risk: High for untracking, since it changes where durable evidence lives and could lose history across clones. Low to medium for TTL alone (a constant plus tests). Because both reverse a recorded decision, the owner must approve. The lock text and decision text agree on the substance, but the spec's lock details are carried as candidate and may be stale (cites: `docs/platform/observe/README.md#added-in-candidate-read-this-area`, `docs/platform/governance.md#4-authority-matrix`).
- Q5 What proof means done: Lock tests for the new TTL and dead-holder reclaim. Doctor checks passing (the spec names `observe-dir-writable` and `observe-friction-migrated`). Regenerated fixtures. A recorded decision. The exact commands are not in scope (cites: `docs/platform/observe/spec.md#contract-quyết-định-làn-c-f8`).
- Q6 What learning gets left behind: A superseding decision (not an in-place edit) in the owner spec's decision history, plus an update of the Storage Model in `docs/specs/observe.md` via a reviewed ratchet exception. Record candidate impact so the candidate copies are re-conserved (cites: `docs/platform/migration-authoring-rules.md#2-legacy-roots-ratchet-and-exceptions`, `docs/platform/migration-authoring-rules.md#1-core-principles`).
