## 3. Disagreements and verdicts

### D-1. `HL-N8 / HL-N9`: Which bytes-equal check to keep in `src/state/hot-lock.mjs:109-137`?
- **Correctness (`T1-CF-03`, `:79-85`):** Keep **both** checks (`reclaimDeadHolder:131` and `unlinkRecordIfUnchanged:111`) and add two multiprocess race tests.
- **Simplicity (`HL-N8/N9`, `:124`):** Keep only `unlinkRecordIfUnchanged:111` and delete `reclaimDeadHolder:130-132` (including `judgeHotRecord(again.record) !== 'dead'`).
- **Speed (`S-6`, `:133`):** In `reclaimDeadHolder` (`:130-133`), read once under `<resource>.reclaim.lock`, verify `again.raw === observed.raw && judgeHotRecord(again.record) === 'dead'`, and `fs.unlinkSync(record)` directly; keep `unlinkRecordIfUnchanged` for `clearHotRecord` (`:147`) only.
- **Verdict:** **Speed (`S-6`) wins.** Under `<resource>.reclaim.lock`, while a dead holder's record exists, `tryLinkRecord` (`:163`) fails with `EEXIST` and all cleaners/fencers hold `<resource>.reclaim.lock`, so the file cannot change between `:131` and `:133` — meaning Correctness's two checks in `reclaimDeadHolder` always cover for each other and cannot be independently pinned. Conversely, Simplicity's deletion of `:130-132` drops `judgeHotRecord(again.record) !== 'dead'` from `reclaimDeadHolder`, violating `hot-lock.v1.json:25`. Speed's split gives one read and one compare per function (`reclaimDeadHolder` vs `clearHotRecord`), each pinned by its own deterministic test (`P-N9` and `P-N8`).

### D-2. `T0-R1`: String-in `canonicalJson(text: String)` door vs benchmarking first
- **Correctness (`T1-CF-06`, `:106-112`):** Benchmark Rust canonicalizer vs JS→`Value` conversion first; do not add a parallel API.
- **Speed (`S-4`, `:41-46, 52-54, 124-125`) & Simplicity (`S-C2`, `:121-122`):** Both measured the split (`63.4 µs` N-API `Value` marshal-in vs `12.1 µs` crate; `from_str` + `canonical_json` is `32.5 µs`) and propose changing `canonical_json.rs:8` to take `text: String`, passing `JSON.stringify(value)` from `door.mjs:74-75`.
- **Verdict:** **Speed and Simplicity's string-in architecture wins on performance (`~81 µs → ~48 µs`), BUT their exact JS code (`const text = JSON.stringify(value)`) is broken** because `JSON.stringify` turns `[1, undefined]`, sparse array holes, and `NaN`/`Infinity` into `"null"` without throwing, failing `test/git/native-load.test.mjs:74` and `component-boundary.md:82`. Passing a 4-line validation replacer to `JSON.stringify` + `if (text === undefined)` in `src/native/door.mjs:74` fixes the bug at `15.04 µs` p50.
