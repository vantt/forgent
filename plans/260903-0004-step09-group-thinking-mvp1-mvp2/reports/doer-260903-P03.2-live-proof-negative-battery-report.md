## The second gap the cell had to close (the one the brief named)

`run.mjs` had no way to call `authorizeDeclaredOperation` or
`recordDriverDisposition`. Closed by two new `steps[]` types on the
existing request shape — no new CLI subcommand, no new verb:

| `type` | Reaches | Fields |
|---|---|---|
| `authorize` | `authorizeDeclaredOperation` (session-engine) | `operationId`, `targetActorId?`, `nodeId?`, `authorizationId`, `invocationKey`, `reason`, `grantedContextRefs?`, `targetArtifactRef?` |
| `disposition` | `recordDriverDisposition` (store) | `targetRef`, `disposition`, `rationale`, `evidenceRefs?` |

Two design points worth your attention:

- **`authorizedBy` is refused, not accepted.** The engine pins both events
  to `manifest.provenanceRoot.writerId`, which is the request's own
  top-level `writerId`, so a request-file `authorizedBy` could only ever
  agree or be refused deeper down — a field with no independent effect,
  exactly what `run.mjs`'s existing fan-out-policy refusal pattern
  forbids. It is refused at the schema boundary with a message naming the
  single real channel. Every other field I accept provably reaches an
  engine call (mutation-proven).
- **`run.mjs` now imports `store.mjs` directly**, for
  `recordDriverDisposition` only — its header comment previously said it
  never does. There is no definition-aware disposition door in
  `session-engine.mjs` to delegate to, and `session-engine.mjs` is Do Not
  Touch for this cell. `store.mjs`'s door is complete on its own (shape
  validation, active-session check, driver-identity pin, lock-held
  idempotent append). I updated the header comment to name the exception
  honestly rather than route around it. If you want the rule back
  literally, it is a one-line re-export from `session-engine.mjs`.
